Stop Eating Like It Costs You: 12 Things You Have Wrong About Your Own Body

There is a version of you that stopped fighting. Probably not this year. Years ago. There was no ceremony. You did not announce it. You just stopped, quietly, somewhere between 42 and 51, maybe older, maybe younger. You told yourself it was maturity.

Here is the part nobody says out loud. Somebody is fine with that. Somebody in your life, maybe two or three people, has already filed you under finished. They are not cruel about it. They do not think about it much at all. They just do not expect anything from your body anymore. They watch you get out of the truck and something on their face goes soft with a kind of pity you would have swung at when you were 26.

I am not going to tell you to prove them wrong. I am going to tell you something worse. On the path you are on right now, today, you are going to prove them right. On schedule. Probably ahead of it.

I am not a doctor. I am not a coach. I am not standing on a stage in a rented suit telling you I cracked the code. I work for a living. I am a 50-something guy who ate in his car so nobody would see him. That is who is talking to you. An addict sitting across from another addict, telling you what I found out the hard way, and it cost me to find out, the same way it is costing you.

What follows is going to hit one of two ways. It is going to make you angry, or it is going to make you tired. If it makes you angry, stay that way. Angry is the last live wire you have got, and we are going to use it.

Twelve things you have wrong. Not motivation. Motivation is a mood, and moods do not survive Tuesday.

Shift 01You are not experiencing age. You are experiencing chemistry.

Get this one clear, because everything else sits on top of it.

The stiffness in your hands in the morning. The forty-five minutes of fog before the coffee lands. The knee. The lower back. The way you have to plan the stairs now. The nap you did not used to need. The mood that drops off a cliff at four in the afternoon.

You filed all of it under one word. Age. And age is the safest word in the English language, because nobody argues with it. You say I am 54 at the dinner table and every head nods. Case closed. No follow-up questions.

Here is the problem with that story. Fifty-four does not spike inflammation. Fifty-four did not put 38 grams of sugar in the thing you drank at two o'clock. Fifty-four has never once in the history of the human body walked into a kitchen at 9:48 at night and opened a cabinet.

Age is not an input. It is a number on a phone. The inputs are what you ate, three to five times a day, for thirty years.

I know what you are doing right now, because I did it. You are running the list. The guy at work who eats worse than you and looks great. Your uncle who smoked. You are building the case.

Stop building the case. You already know the answer. You know it at three in the morning when you wake for no reason and lie there doing the math on your own life. That is the only hour you tell yourself the truth.

You are not old. You are inflamed, insulin resistant, underslept, overfed and chemically hooked. Every one of those is reversible. Age is not. That is the whole point. You have been blaming the one thing you cannot change for damage caused by the things you can.

Shift 02That is not hunger at 9:30. That is withdrawal.

Dinner was at 6:30. You ate real food, a decent portion, and you were full when you pushed back from the table. Three hours later you are standing in front of the refrigerator with the door open, looking. Not for anything specific. Just looking. You feel the cold air on your chest, one hand on the door, scanning shelves you have already scanned twice.

You are not hungry. Understand me. I do not mean you are not very hungry. I mean the hunger signal is not firing at all. It is not biologically possible for you to be in caloric need three hours after a full meal while standing on a hundred thousand calories of stored energy strapped to your belly and your face.

So what is it? It is withdrawal. It is your brain, trained over two decades to expect a hit at a certain hour, sending up a flare. And because nobody ever taught you to read that signal, you translate it into the only word you have got. Hungry.

You have been in withdrawal every night for years, and you have been medicating it with the exact substance that caused it.

Shift 03You did not lose to a snack. You got out-engineered.

The food industry does not stumble into this. It is not an accident of the market. There are people with doctorates in neuroscience and behavioral psychology whose entire job is to find the exact ratio of sugar, fat, salt and crunch that overrides the switch in your brain that says stop.

They call it a bliss point. That is not my phrase. That is theirs. It is in their literature. They tested it on rats and then they tested it on you. They engineered products to beat your biology on purpose, and then they sold you the idea that when you lose to those products, it is a character flaw.

You are not weak. You got out-engineered by a multi-billion-dollar research operation with a thirty-year head start and no rules.

Now here is the second half, and this is where I stop letting you off the hook. Knowing you got engineered does not make it not your problem anymore. The war is on your kitchen counter and nobody else is coming to fight it there. Not the government, not the FDA, not your doctor, not your wife or your husband. That is your counter, your hands, your mouth. They set the trap. You are still the only one who can walk away from it.

Shift 04You do not have to heal the inside first. That order is backwards.

Somewhere along the way you picked up an idea that sounds compassionate and evolved, and it has probably cost you ten years. The idea is this: I have to fix what is broken inside me before I can fix what is broken outside me. Find the root cause. Get to why you eat. Work through the thing with your father underneath it. Then, once you are healed, the body will follow.

Let me be careful here, so hear this one thing. If you are in therapy, stay in therapy. If you have real trauma and you are doing that work with a professional, that is not weakness, that is maintenance on the most valuable equipment you own. I respect the hell out of it. Keep going. I mean that.

But do not let it be the gate. Because as a gate it is a beautiful trap, the most comfortable one ever built for a man like you. As long as the healing has to come first, the eating never has to change today. You get to feel serious about yourself while you order the same thing you ordered last Friday. You have turned self-work into a waiting room.

And you might not have this time. Do the actual arithmetic on how many high-function years are in front of you if nothing changes. Not lifespan. Healthspan. Years where you can pick up a grandkid, carry your own bag, sleep through the night, get up off the floor without a plan. You do not get to spend six of those in a waiting room working your way toward being emotionally ready to eat an egg.

Here is the actual shift, and it runs the other way. The body is not downstream of the mind. Not entirely, and not even mostly. When your blood sugar stops slamming up and crashing down eight times a day, your mood stabilizes. When systemic inflammation drops, the fog lifts. When you sleep deep instead of shallow, your emotional regulation comes back online. When you keep one promise to yourself fourteen days straight, self-respect returns, and it does not return as a feeling. It returns as evidence.

People walk into therapy carrying anxiety, low mood and no drive, and a real percentage of that is not psychological. It is metabolic. It is chemistry that got labeled character.

I am not telling you food fixes your childhood. I am telling you that you will do the hard inside work better and faster with a clear head, from inside a body that is not constantly on fire. So flip the order. Do not heal, then eat. Eat and heal at the same time, starting tonight, in the body you actually have.

Shift 05Normal for your age is the most dangerous sentence in American medicine.

You go in once a year. You sit on the paper and it crinkles. They take the blood, they take the pressure, they come back in and they say it: for your age, these numbers are pretty normal.

Normal compared to what? Compared to a population where roughly three out of four adults are overweight or obese. Where somewhere around one in three has a fatty liver. Where over a hundred million people are diabetic or pre-diabetic and most of them have not been told. That is the reference range. That is the curve you just got graded on.

You did not get told you were healthy. You got told you were average. And average is on fire.

Now let me be square with you, because I am not going to sell you a lie to make a point. I am not a doctor. I do not have a prescription. If you are on blood pressure medication, a statin, metformin, do not stop taking it because a guy with a camera got loud. That is not courage. That is stupid, and it can kill you.

Here is what a man does instead. You go back in, you sit down, and you say this out loud: I am changing what I eat, all of it, starting right now. I want a full panel today as a baseline, and I want to be retested in 90 days. When those numbers move, I want you to walk me down off these medications on your schedule, with your supervision.

That is not defiance. That is command presence in a doctor's office.

Because here is what is not in the pamphlet. Most of them will not open that conversation with you. Not because they are corrupt, but because they have eleven minutes, a full waiting room, and thirty years of evidence that men your age do not follow through anyway. They are not lying to you. They have just stopped expecting anything from you. Same as everybody else.

Shift 06You did earn something. You collected the wrong thing.

Listen for this word in your own mouth this week. It is going to show up more than you think. Deserve.

I worked 60 hours this week, I deserve a night off. I am 55 years old, I have earned the right to enjoy myself.

Every one of those sentences is true. And every one of them is being used against you, by you. Look at what you are attaching it to. You worked thirty years. You raised the kids. You made the payments. You showed up when you did not want to. That is real. You did earn something.

So what did you decide to collect? A substance that inflames your joints, fogs your brain, wrecks your sleep, and adds two more years to how long it takes you to get up off the floor. That is not a reward. That is a fine you volunteered to pay.

Run the word all the way out and see if you still like it. Do you deserve to be the guy your kids have to plan around? Do you deserve to be the one who cannot do the hike, so nobody books the hike? Do you deserve to be the man your wife starts quietly managing instead of leaning on? Do you deserve a last decade spent in waiting rooms and parking structures, on the phone with a pharmacy, on a schedule set by other people?

Because that is the actual package. The pastry is just the trailer.

You do deserve better. You are right about that. You just have the wrong list. You deserve to walk into a room and have it register. You deserve a mind that stays sharp through a four o'clock meeting. You deserve to be the strongest man on your street at 58, which, and I want you to hear how low the bar is, is a nearly empty category.

You have been trading a thirty-year career for a thirty-second flavor.

Shift 07You are not giving anything up. You are cancelling a subscription.

This is the one that actually stops men. You start thinking about doing this and immediately you start grieving. The ketchup. The mayonnaise. The dressing on the salad. The oil you cook everything in. Bread with dinner, bread to sop up the sauce. Something fried on Friday. The pastry with the coffee. The ice cream. The chips. The thing you eat standing up in the kitchen that you do not even count, because you did not sit down for it.

You feel the loss in advance. That is real. I felt it. I still feel that pull. It is a genuine grief response and it is worth naming instead of pretending you are going to escape it.

So here is what you do with it. Do the math on what you are already losing.

Look down. Right now, sitting there. What did those things buy you? That is not a metaphor, that is the receipt. Every item is on it. The ketchup is on the receipt. The oil is on there somewhere. Twenty years of Friday nights are on there. It is all itemized, and you are wearing it.

Now run it forward. Six months from now you are eating beef, chicken, eggs, fish and vegetables. Nothing with a label, nothing in a wrapper. You look down. What are you missing? Not what did you give up. What are you missing?

Nothing. You are going to look down at a body that works and try to remember what was so important about a condiment.

Hold onto that, because your brain will try to run the old math on you. The old math says I am giving something up. The new math says I am done paying for something I never wanted. You are not on a diet. Nobody is taking anything from you. You are cancelling a subscription you did not know you had, one that has been billing you for twenty years in joints and sleep and dignity. Nobody grieves a cancelled subscription.

Shift 08The protocol fits on a business card.

Here is where people expect the complicated part. The macros. The tracking app. The scale. The eating window. The plan.

There is no complicated part.

The whole protocol

Beef, chicken, eggs, fish, vegetables. That is it. You can put it on a business card and have room left over.

If it has an ingredient list, do not eat it. Not because ingredient lists are evil, but because of what they mean. An ingredient list means something got assembled. It means a company made a series of decisions about what would keep you coming back, and not one of those decisions was made in your interest.

A ribeye does not have an ingredient list. An egg does not have an ingredient list. Broccoli has never had a nutrition label negotiated.

One question, three seconds. At the store, at the drive-thru, at your mother-in-law's house: did this come out of the ground or off an animal, or did it come off a line?

Now let me kill the objection you are holding right now. You think this is going to be miserable. You are picturing plain chicken breast in a plastic container, steamed broccoli, no salt, eaten with resentment at your desk.

That is not this food. That is what bodybuilders eat when they are cutting and punishing themselves. You are not a bodybuilder.

This is a ribeye with a hard sear and salt. Eggs in butter. Thighs with the skin crisped. Salmon skin-down on a hot pan. Real bacon, no sugar in the cure. Steak and eggs at seven in the morning with no plan for lunch, because you are not hungry until two.

The stuff you are grieving is not the delicious stuff. It only tastes like that because chemists made it taste like that, and it only feels necessary because it made itself necessary. Twenty days in, real food tastes like more than it used to and the old stuff tastes like a chemical. I did not believe that either. Then it happened.

Shift 09The cheat day is the most expensive thing on your schedule.

You string five clean days together. Maybe seven. You feel it starting to work. The pants sit differently. You slept through the night for the first time in a while.

And what is the very first thing your brain does? It starts looking for the off-ramp. I have earned Saturday. One day is not going to hurt. You have to live a little. You cannot eat like this forever.

Listen to that voice. Not the words, the tone. That is not you talking. That is the thing talking, and it is using your voice, and it is being extremely reasonable, and it is telling you it only wants one afternoon. It is exactly what it said the last 400 times.

Move it one category over and hear how insane it sounds. Nobody looks at a guy who is nine days off the bottle and says great job, go get hammered on Saturday, you earned it. Nobody hands a man who just quit smoking a pack on Friday as a reward for Monday through Thursday. We understand it perfectly with every other substance. We understand that the reward cannot be the substance. That is not controversial in any room.

But with food we invented a whole culture around it and gave it a friendly name. Cheat day. That is the word we picked, and we say it like we are getting away with something. Who exactly are you cheating? There is nobody else in this. Nobody gets fooled. Nobody loses but you.

And here is what it actually does mechanically, which is worse than the calories. It resets the negotiation. For five days you were a man who does not eat that. Full stop. Closed question. On Saturday afternoon you became a man who sometimes eats that. Now every single day for the rest of your life is a negotiation, because you proved the door can be opened.

You did not eat a pizza. You reopened a case you had already closed. Closed questions are easy. Open questions will exhaust you until you quit.

Shift 10Your kids are not watching your weight. They are learning a shape.

This is the one that got me, so I am going to say it straight.

You have told your kids to finish what they start. You have told them nobody is coming to save them. You have told them discipline is showing up when you do not feel like it. That is your material. You have delivered it more than once and you meant every word.

And they have watched you announce a new start out loud at the table. And then they watched Saturday. And they watched you announce it again on January 1st. And then Saturday.

They are not keeping a ledger to be cruel. They are not thinking about it much at all. But there is a file in their head with your name on it, and every one of those cycles went in. What they took from it is not Dad struggles with food. What they took is the shape: declare hard, unfold quietly, say nothing about it, restart later. That is a complete behavioral template, and you did not teach it with words. You taught it with your body, over years. They will run it on their own hard thing. Their business. Their marriage. Their own quit.

That is the inheritance nobody puts in a will.

Now flip it, because it cuts the other way just as hard. Imagine what it does to a 26-year-old to watch their father, at 50-something, with everything against him, thirty years of habit, bad knees, a full schedule, no support and nobody expecting a thing, decide one Tuesday and then never break. Not talk about it. Do it. For a year. Quietly.

They will never forget that. It may become the thing they reach for at their own worst hour. My old man did it at 56 with nothing going for him. And they will never tell you that is what they were thinking about. You do not get the credit in real time. You do not need the credit.

It is the highest-leverage thing left available to you, and it does not require money, a gym, a coach, or a single conversation. It just requires you not eating something.

Shift 11Being average is not safety. It is camouflage.

You look around and you feel fine about yourself. Bob has a gut. Dave is on pills. Jim got a stent last year. Everybody at the barbecue looks about like you look. So you are fine. You are mid-pack.

You are mid-pack in a pack that is dying.

Look at where the pack is actually going, because the belt only moves one direction and nobody gets off it. It starts at the grocery aisle. It moves to the annual physical. Then the first dose, just to get the number down. Then a second one. Then a specialist. Then a procedure. Then a walker in the garage that everybody pretends is temporary. Then a facility with a name that sounds like a resort.

Every man at that barbecue is on it, at a different mile marker, and not one of them has ever seriously considered stepping off, because everybody they know is on the same belt.

Being average in that group does not make you safe. It hides the fact that you are in the group, in the same line, at the same speed, holding the same ticket. That comparison is the coward's comparison. I have made it, so I know exactly what it is for. It is not information. It is anesthesia.

Stop comparing yourself to the men who quit. Compare yourself to the man you were at 31, and go get him back. He is not gone. He is buried, and that is a completely different problem with a completely different solution.

Shift 12You are not a man who keeps failing. You are a man who has not stopped attempting.

Here is the real objection, the one under everything. You have tried. Not once, not twice. More times than you would say out loud, and every one of those failures is sitting in the room with you right now, and their whole job is to tell you this attempt ends the same way.

So let me hand you the only thing that ever helped me.

Think about something you actually did quit. Cigarettes. The bottle. Chew. Something you finally put down for good and never picked back up. Now tell me why that time worked.

I bet you cannot. Nobody can. It was not the best-planned attempt. It was not the one with the most support, or the best timing, or the strongest motivation. Half the time it was a random Tuesday with nothing special about it at all. You had quit forty times with a plan, and the one that stuck did not have one.

Here is what that means, and I think it is the most useful thing in this whole piece. You cannot identify the successful attempt in advance. It does not feel different when it starts. It only becomes the one that worked in retrospect, after enough time passes.

Which means every attempt you made was not a failure. It was a required attempt in a sequence you cannot see the end of. You are not a guy who keeps failing. You are a guy who has not stopped attempting. Those are opposite men wearing the same face.

And this is the fork. The only way you guarantee you never hit the one that works is to stop attempting. That is the only losing move. Not relapse. Quitting the sequence.

So this one, the one you are thinking about right now because of this: it might be it. It might not. Start it anyway. Because attempt number 401 is not available to a man who quit at 40.

You have not hit critical mass

You think you have blown it. The damage is done, the back is shot, the knees are gone, twenty years of this, might as well ride it out.

Here is the actual clinical picture, and it is better news than you have allowed yourself. The liver regenerates fast. Fatty liver reverses, and it reverses quickly, in weeks rather than years, when you cut the fructose and the seed oils and the alcohol. Insulin sensitivity comes back with remarkable speed once you stop hammering it six times a day. Blood pressure moves inside a month. Systemic inflammation drops in a couple of weeks. Your skin turns over. Your gut lining turns over.

Your body is not a car with 190,000 miles on it. It is a construction site that has been receiving garbage materials for two decades and never stopped building. Give it steel and it builds with steel, starting with tonight's delivery.

But hear the second half of that, because I am not going to hand you comfort and walk away. Not yet is not the same as not ever. There is a line and it is real and it has a date on it you do not get to see. It is the amputation. It is the event on the kitchen floor. It is the scan that comes back with a word on it that reorganizes your entire family for a year.

You are not past it. You are walking toward it at a known speed, and you have just been handed the exact location of the brake pedal. That is not a scare tactic. That is the most optimistic thing here. Everything is still available to you today. Not in the spring. Not after the holidays. Not Monday. Monday is the oldest lie in this thing, and the calendar will keep issuing you a fresh one every seven days until you are in the box.

What you actually do tonight

It is short, because it has to be small enough to survive a bad day.

One. Tonight, not tomorrow night.

Walk into the kitchen and remove everything with an ingredient list. All of it. Not eat it down so it does not go to waste, that is the addiction talking and it sounds thrifty. Bag it out. The forty dollars you throw away tonight is the cheapest thing you will buy all year. And if you live with people, they should not be eating that either. You are running this show.

Two. Tomorrow you eat.

Beef, chicken, eggs or fish, and some vegetables. No label, no wrapper, no drive-thru, no exception. Eat as much as you want. Do not count anything, do not weigh anything, do not download an app. Salt it, cook it right, eat it.

Three. When 9:30 hits, name it out loud.

It is going to hit, and it is going to be loud. Stand in the kitchen with nobody there and say it: this is withdrawal, this is not hunger. Then drink water and go to bed. That is the entire skill. 9:30 is the whole war and it lasts about eleven minutes.

Four. Take the picture tomorrow morning.

Shirt off, bad lighting, the one you do not want to take. Nobody sees it. You are not building content and you are not starting a channel. You are creating the before that makes lying to yourself impossible in six weeks.

Five. Tell one person. One.

Not a Facebook announcement. One human being who will look you in the eye and ask you about it. Or tell nobody. Those are the two options.

Days one through three are chemical. You will get a headache. You will be irritable. You will feel like it is not working. That is not the food failing. That is the substance leaving. It ends. Everybody tells you day four.

The best sentence available to men our age

Somebody has already decided how the rest of your life goes. Somebody watched you get older and softer and quieter and made a call about who you are now. And they were not wrong to make it, because you handed them the evidence.

Do not give them the satisfaction. And do not do it with an announcement at the table about turning over a new leaf. Nobody needs to hear that.

Just do not eat it. That is the whole rebellion. It is not loud. Nobody will notice for a few weeks. Then everybody will notice and nobody will say anything, until one of them finally asks. And then you get to say the best sentence available to men our age.

I stopped.

You do not have to fix your childhood first. You do not have to wait until January. You do not need a coach, or a Monday, or a gym, or a supplement, or a program, or one more piece of information. You have all the information you need and you have had it for years. You need to not open that cabinet at 9:30 tonight. Everything else is downstream of that.

I am doing this with you, not ahead of you. Same cabinet, same hour, same fight, tonight.

The daily work, the log, and the men doing it in real time. No fads. Nobody selling you powder.

Go there tonight

Then look down. In six months you are going to look down and try to remember what you thought you were going to miss.

The answer is nothing. You are going to miss nothing.

Your brain was trained over two decades to expect a hit at a certain hour and it sends up a flare. The only word most people have for that flare is hungry, so that is what they call it, and then they medicate it with the substance that caused it.
